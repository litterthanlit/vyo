# Exploded View Layout (Page 3)

Section spec for `<repo-name>-exploded-view.canvas.tsx`. Read [swiss-design-principles.md](swiss-design-principles.md) first, including its [page 3 drawing exception](swiss-design-principles.md#page-3-drawing-exception).

Page 3 draws **this repo's real parts and real wiring** as an isometric exploded view. Each part is a shape chosen by what it is (screens, features, AI, background jobs, saved data, outside services), sized from the scan, stacked in height tiers and joined by cables that exist in the code. A reader drags a slider to pull the tiers apart and points at a part to read what it does. It answers the question pages 1 and 2 can't answer visually: *what is this made of, and what talks to what?*

Every repo looks different because every repo is different. Nothing on this page is chosen for looks: shapes come from roles, sizes from the scan, cables from imports and calls.

|------|------|-----|
| 3 — Exploded View | `<repo-name>-exploded-view.canvas.tsx` | The app's real parts and connections, one plain sentence per part |

## From codebase to parts

```
repo ──scan──▶ <repo>-facts.json ──you write PARTS──▶ wire ──▶ WIRING block ──▶ the canvas draws it
```

| Step | Command (run from the skill directory) | Result |
|------|----------------------------------------|--------|
| Scan | `node tools/scan.mjs scan <repo> --out <canvases>/<repo>-facts.json` | Facts: screens, endpoints, tables, outside services, AI, jobs, folders with their share of the code, imports |
| Read | `node tools/scan.mjs show <facts.json>` | A plain summary to work from. Do not re-read the repo |
| Draft | `node tools/scan.mjs draft <facts.json>` | A suggested `PARTS` array (valid JSON) to edit |
| Group | edit `PARTS` in the canvas | Your job: names, roles, lines, `from` |
| Wire | `node tools/scan.mjs wire <facts.json> --into <canvas>` | Rewrites the `WIRING` block. `✗` = honesty error, `!` = something to weigh |
| Check a cable | `node tools/scan.mjs why <facts.json> --canvas <canvas> "A" "B"` | One real file path behind a link |

Who decides what:

| Who | Decides |
|-----|---------|
| Scanner | What exists: facts, folders, which file imports which |
| You | Which facts form a part, its plain name, role and sentence |
| `wire` | Counts, sizes and links. You never type a number |
| The starter | Every coordinate, shape, cable and label position |

The canvas holds two marked blocks. Everything outside them is the starter.

```ts
// PARTS:BEGIN — you write this (valid JSON). Then run: node tools/scan.mjs wire <facts.json> --into <this file>
const PARTS: PartSpec[] = [
  {"name": "What you see", "role": "screens", "line": "When you open Pantry, you move between your recipes, the weekly plan and your settings.", "from": ["screen:**"]}
];
// PARTS:END
// WIRING:BEGIN — generated by `node tools/scan.mjs wire`; do not edit
const WIRING: Wiring = {"facts":"…","parts":"…","n":[…],"links":[…]};
// WIRING:END
```

### Rules for `PARTS`

- **2–9 parts.** Two is a warning. One, or ten or more, is an error.
- **`name`**: 18 characters at most, in a user's words ([naming](plain-language-rules.md#naming-parts)). **`line`**: one sentence, "When you X, the app Y".
- **`role`** is one of the seven in the table below. It picks the shape and decides which facts the part may cite.
- **`from`** lists patterns that each match a whole fact id (`screen:/plan`, `table:Recipe`, `service:payments`, `ai:openai`, `job:send-digest`, `area:lib/ai`; read them in `facts.json` or `draft`). `*` stops at `/`, `**` does not, and a leading `!` removes matches. Patterns apply in order.
- **Every positive pattern must match at least one fact.** A miss prints the nearest real ids.
- **A fact belongs to one part.** No id may be cited twice. The same `area:` may not appear in two parts (nested areas are fine; the deepest wins).
- **A part must count something.** A non-feature part with nothing to count is an error.
- **Any role may also cite `area:` folders** to claim the code in them. That changes which part owns a file, so it changes the cables, but never the counts. A `feature` part cites only `area:`.

## Roles, shapes and units

Each drawn unit is one mark you can point at. When there are more facts than the cap, the last unit is a bundle for all the rest: the label keeps the true count and the caption says "and N more".

| `role` | Shape | One unit per… | What it stands for in the code (`from` cites) | Cap |
|--------|-------|---------------|-----------------------------------------------|-----|
| `screens` | Portrait cards standing in a shallow tray, spread in a row | screen | Pages a person visits (`screen:`) | 12 |
| `api` | Low patch panel with sockets in rows of four | kind of request | What other programs can ask the app to do (`endpoint:`) | 16 |
| `feature` | A block that grows with its share of the code, chips on top | sub-folder of related code | A feature's folders (`area:` only). One folder → its sub-folders; several folders → those folders | 6 |
| `ai` | Hexagonal prism ringed by small satellites, one per AI tool (up to 6) | AI provider | AI models the app calls (`ai:`) | 4 |
| `jobs` | Flat gears, meshed | job that runs on its own | Scheduled and background work (`job:`) | 4 |
| `data` | Cylinder, a stack of disks | kind of saved record | What the app saves (`table:`) | 10 |
| `service` | Plug block with prongs | outside service | Companies the app plugs into, named by what they do for you (`service:`) | 6 |

Labels (written by the drawing, never by you) read "6 screens", "Answers 3 kinds of request", "40% of the code", "1 AI model · 2 tools", "Runs 1 job on its own", "4 kinds of saved record", "3 outside services".

## Choosing parts

Start from `draft`, which cites the scanner's balanced split of the folders. Then make each part something a non-technical person would point at.

- **A part per recognisable thing, not per folder.** "Recipe box", "Billing", "Weekly email". If you can't say what it does for a user in one sentence, it is not a part.
- **Aim for 4 to 7.** Two is thin. Past nine the drawing is a parts list.
- **Merge small areas.** Folders that hold a few percent of the code (see the shares in `show`) fold into the nearest feature by adding them to its `from`, or stay uncited. Uncited code is not lost: cables run through it.
- **Split a feature** when one folder holds a large share of the code (about a third or more) and its sub-folders have names a person would say aloud. Cite the sub-folders as separate parts ("Billing", "Accounts").
- **Leave endpoints uncited when they are only glue** between screens and features. The cables then run screen → request → feature, which is the truth. Add an `api` part when the API is the product (no screens, or other programs are the customers).
- **Fold services into one part** ("Outside services") when they are small plugs. Give a service its own part when users meet it by name ("Payments", "Sign-in") or when there are more than six.
- **Tables are usually one `data` part.** Split only when users name the groups differently ("Saved recipes" and "Your account").
- **Group by what works today.** Backend-only or planned work is excluded with a `!` pattern (`"service:*", "!service:payments"`) and named in the footer, the same list as pages 1 and 2.
- **Read the `!` warnings.** "Not drawn: Payments (service), 2 tables" means the drawing hides those. Draw them or leave them out on purpose.

## Tiers

Height comes from the role. You do not place parts.

| Tier | Roles |
|------|-------|
| 1 (top) | `screens`, `api` |
| 2 | `feature` |
| 3 | `ai`, `jobs` |
| 4 (bottom) | `data`, `service` |

- **Empty tiers disappear**, so an API-only service has two tiers where a full app has four.
- **Tier 3 folds into tier 2** when the two together hold four parts or fewer.
- **Four parts per tier at most.** A fifth throws `Tier with <n> parts — merge parts`.
- **Order inside a tier** is computed from the cables, so connected parts sit near each other.

## Grid rows

Same outer 12-column grid, 8px baseline and 64px margins as pages 1 and 2.

| Row | Columns | Content |
|-----|---------|---------|
| Folio + 2px rule | 12 | "Exploded View — 03" · "The app, taken apart" · product name |
| Masthead | 1–12 | Product name, 112px, red full stop — same as page 1 |
| Purpose | 1–7 | One sentence on what the drawing shows. Cols 8–12 stay empty |
| Section label | 1–12 | "Taken apart — drag to open, point at a part to read it" |
| Figure | 1–12 | `<figure>`: the drawing in cols 3–10; part labels in the gutters, left parts in cols 1–2 and right parts in cols 11–12, each on a column line with a hairline leader |
| Caption | 1–8 | Selected part: folio "02 / 07 — Recipe box", its line, "Made of: …", "Connected to: …" (`aria-live`) |
| Controls | 1–12 | "Open layers" slider · percent · theme button (hidden in exports) |
| Footer | 12 | Not built yet · audience · Show grid |

- **Labels never wrap.** Line 1 is a folio (`01 — RECIPE BOX`), line 2 is the count phrase. This is why names stop at 18 characters.
- The drawing is at most **704px** tall, fixed from its open state so the page never jumps. If the parts can't fit, the canvas throws; merge parts.
- The footer lists what is not built, plus any important fact you left out on purpose.

## Interaction

| Input | Result |
|-------|--------|
| Drag the slider, or ←/→/Home/End on it | Opens and closes the tiers |
| Point at or click a part or its label | Selects it: tint fill, ink outline, its cables turn ink, caption updates |
| Tab to the parts list, then ↑/↓ (Home/End) | Moves the selection through the parts |
| Theme button | Light paper ↔ dark blueprint |
| Show grid | The usual grid overlay |

State lives in `useCanvasState` under page 3 keys: `openExploded` (80), `partExploded` (0), `themeExploded` ("light") and `showGridExploded` (false). Never reuse page 1 or page 2 keys. Exports use the defaults: light, 80% open, part 01 selected.

## Worked example: Pantry

Pantry is the recipe test fixture: a Next.js app with six screens, three endpoints, one weekly cron job, four tables (User, Recipe, Ingredient, MealPlan), three outside services (payments, email, sign-in) and one OpenAI integration with two AI tools. The seven parts:

```json
[
  {"name": "What you see", "role": "screens", "line": "When you open Pantry, you move between your recipes, the weekly plan and your settings.", "from": ["screen:**"]},
  {"name": "Recipe box", "role": "feature", "line": "When you save a recipe, Pantry keeps it in your box so you can find it, change it and cook it again.", "from": ["area:lib/recipes", "area:components/recipes"]},
  {"name": "Meal planner", "role": "feature", "line": "When you plan your week, Pantry puts your recipes on the days you choose.", "from": ["area:lib/plan", "area:components/plan"]},
  {"name": "Recipe ideas", "role": "ai", "line": "When you ask for ideas, an AI model suggests recipes for you to try.", "from": ["ai:*", "area:lib/ai"]},
  {"name": "Weekly email", "role": "jobs", "line": "Once a week, on its own, Pantry prepares your meal plan and emails it to you.", "from": ["job:**"]},
  {"name": "Saved recipes", "role": "data", "line": "When you save a recipe, a plan or your details, it is kept here, so it is waiting next time you come back.", "from": ["table:*"]},
  {"name": "Outside services", "role": "service", "line": "When Pantry takes payment, sends mail or checks who you are, it hands that job to an outside company.", "from": ["service:*"]}
]
```

| Part | Shape | Counts | Why it is shaped this way |
|------|-------|--------|---------------------------|
| What you see | Tray of cards | 6 screens | One card per screen the scan found |
| Recipe box | Block with chips | share of the code | Two folders (the logic and the pieces of screen that show it) read as one thing to a user |
| Meal planner | Block with chips | share of the code | Same, for planning |
| Recipe ideas | Prism with 2 satellites | 1 AI model · 2 tools | The folder `lib/ai` is claimed so its code belongs to this part |
| Weekly email | One gear | 1 job | The cron job is a job, not an endpoint |
| Saved recipes | Cylinder, 4 disks | 4 kinds of saved record | All four tables in one part |
| Outside services | Plug block, 3 prongs | 3 outside services | Small plugs, folded into one part |

- **Tiers:** screens on top; then Recipe box, Meal planner, Recipe ideas and Weekly email in one tier (the AI/jobs tier folds in, 2 + 2 ≤ 4); then Saved recipes and Outside services at the bottom. Three tiers.
- **No `api` part.** The three endpoints stay uncited. The screens' fetch calls to `/api/recipes` and `/api/suggest` are followed into their handlers, so they end as cables to whichever part owns the code behind them.
- **Name vs. role.** "Weekly email" is a `jobs` part named for what it does for the user. Neither "cron" nor "Resend" appears.

## What not to do

- Don't hand-edit `WIRING`, and don't type counts, sizes or links anywhere. Change `from` or `role`, then re-run `wire`.
- Don't pad. A part with nothing behind it, or a tier added for balance, makes the drawing say something untrue.
- Don't name a part after a technology ("Prisma", "Next.js", "tRPC") or a role word ("API", "Endpoints", "Database tables", "Components"). "Outside services" is plain English and fine. See [plain-language-rules.md](plain-language-rules.md#naming-parts).
- Don't put numbers in a `line`; the label already carries the count and a stale one is an invented count.
- Don't put SVG `<text>` in the drawing. Labels are HTML, so they stay on the type scale and inside the checker.
- Don't colour parts by meaning (blue for data, green for screens). The only colour is the tint on the selected part.
- Don't hand-edit coordinates, sizes or label positions. Change the facts you cite and let the starter lay it out.
